Findlay is the county seat of Hancock County and the base for this service. The 2020 Census counted 40,313 residents in the city, and the surrounding area is a mix of city neighborhoods, small villages and farmland. Dispatch is open 24 hours a day, every day, and the crew heading to you depends on where you are and what else is happening that day.

The address on file is 1800 Tiffin Ave, Findlay, OH 45840. Because we do not promise a fixed arrival time, call first and describe your situation. Dispatch will tell you what to do while help is on the way, which usually starts with stopping the water.

Findlay neighborhoods

Within Findlay, the work is much the same wherever you are: extraction, drying and rebuild, adapted to the building. Properties near the Blanchard River face river flooding questions that homes farther away do not.

The National Weather Service recorded that heavy rain from August 19 to 21, 2007 sent the river near its record stage of 18.5 feet set in 1913, and that history is why flood coverage and floodplain rules come up here so often.

How coverage works across the county

Hancock County is a mix of the city of Findlay, small villages and open farmland. Water damage looks different in each. A city rowhouse shares walls and drains with its neighbors, a village home may be older with its own plumbing history, and a rural property may be far from the nearest dispatch point. The first step is the same everywhere. Stop the water if it is safe, and call.

Because conditions vary, we do not publish fixed arrival times for any town. What we can tell you is what dispatch will do on the call. It will confirm your address, ask about the source and the rooms involved, tell you what to do while you wait and arrange a crew. If your address is far from town, say so at the start.

Choosing what to do in the first ten minutes

  1. Make sure everyone is safe and out of standing water near electricity.
  2. Stop the source, such as a main valve, an appliance supply or a running tap.
  3. Take photos and a short video of the damage before anything is moved.
  4. Call dispatch and describe the water, the rooms and the source.
  5. Move small valuables and papers to a dry room, and leave heavy items for the crew.

These five steps apply in every town we serve. They keep people safe, limit the spread and give the crew and your insurer a clean record to work from.

Weather and seasons in the area

Findlay averages about 38 inches of precipitation and about 24 inches of snow a year, according to NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals, and its record low is minus 21 degrees Fahrenheit. Winter brings frozen pipes and thaw leaks. Spring and summer bring heavy rain, sump pump failures and river flooding. Each season shapes the calls dispatch receives.

The same weather affects the whole county. A storm that floods a Findlay basement can also stress a village sewer, and a hard freeze that splits pipes in town can do the same in a farmhouse. Check the town pages below for local notes.

What to have ready when you call

  1. Your address and the nearest cross street.
  2. Where the water is coming from, if you know, and whether it has been stopped.
  3. Which rooms are wet and roughly how much water is standing.
  4. Whether the water looks clean, gray or like sewage.
  5. Whether any electrical outlets or appliances are in or near the water.

Distances above are approximate and measured from Findlay. They are not arrival times. If you live outside the towns listed, call anyway, since dispatch can tell you whether your address can be served.
