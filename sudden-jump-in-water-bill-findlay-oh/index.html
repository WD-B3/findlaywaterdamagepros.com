Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill? How to Check for a Hidden Leak in Findlay, OH

A bill that jumps for no clear reason is often the first sign of a leak inside a wall, under a slab or in a toilet. A ten-minute meter test tells you a lot.

☎ (567) 406-9153

Water bills usually move slowly with the seasons. When one jumps and nothing changed in the house, a leak is the likeliest explanation. The trouble is that the worst leaks are the ones you cannot see: a supply line in a wall, a pipe under a slab, a toilet flapper that lets water trickle all day.

You can narrow it down in about ten minutes without tools. If the test points to a leak, a plumber finds and fixes the pipe, and a restoration crew deals with what the water already reached. They are different jobs and you may need both.

The ten-minute meter test

  1. Turn off every tap and appliance that uses water. Make sure no one runs a washer, dishwasher or ice maker.
  2. Find your water meter, usually in a basement, a utility room or a box near the street.
  3. Look at the leak indicator, which is often a small triangle or dial that spins when any water moves. Note the reading.
  4. Wait ten to fifteen minutes without using water.
  5. Check again. If the indicator moves or the numbers have changed, water is flowing somewhere.

If you have a main shutoff, close it and check again. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house, which is outdoors or underground.

When damage is already there

A leak that has run for weeks can soak insulation, drywall and subflooring. If you find damp materials, the repair of the pipe is only part of the job. The wet materials still have to be dried or removed. The slab leak page and the wall and drywall page explain what that looks like.

Billing and insurance questions

If a hidden leak is the cause of a high bill, ask your water provider whether it has an adjustment process for leaks, since policies differ. Ask your insurer about coverage for the damage and whether the leaking pipe itself is covered. Many policies treat a long-running leak differently from a sudden break, so document when you found it.

What a leak looks like in the numbers

Compare this bill with the same month last year, not with last month. Water use varies with the season, guests and lawn care. A jump that cannot be explained by any of those, and that persists on the next bill, is worth testing.

If your household has one person away for a week and the bill still rises, a leak is more likely than a habit. Likewise, if the meter indicator moves in the night when everyone is asleep and nothing is running, water is going somewhere.

After the leak is found

Once a plumber has fixed the pipe, check the area for signs of damage. Look for stained drywall, soft flooring and a musty smell. If you find them, document them with photos and dates, and call a crew to measure moisture. The sooner wet materials are dried, the fewer have to be replaced.

Leaks that do not show on the meter at night

Some leaks only run when the system is under use, such as a hot water line that leaks when the heater cycles or an irrigation zone that leaks only when it runs. If the overnight meter test is clean but the bill is high, repeat it at a different time of day and with each appliance turned on in turn.

If you cannot find the leak, a plumber with leak detection tools can narrow it down without opening walls. Tell the plumber what the meter test showed, since that helps find the right line faster.

What to do first while you hunt for a leak

Do not cut into walls or break up a floor to look for a leak. Shut off the main valve if you suspect a serious one. Stay out of water near outlets and keep children away from wet floors. A plumber with detection tools can find most leaks without demolition.

Findlay note: who bills you

The City of Findlay operates its water plant and distribution system, with two reservoirs in Marion Township, and the plant serves Findlay and the surrounding area, according to the city's water page.

The city also provides water and wastewater service to the village of Van Buren, so a Van Buren resident with a billing question can start with the city's water department as well. If you are not sure who bills your address, check the bill itself or call the city.

Before any digging to find an outdoor leak, call 811, which the Ohio Utilities Protection Service answers at 1-800-362-2764, so buried lines are marked. For homes in Van Buren, McComb or Arlington, the billing provider may differ, so check your statement first.

A local question answered

How do I know if my meter shows a leak?

Shut off all water use and watch the leak indicator or the dial for ten to fifteen minutes. Movement means water is flowing.

Can a toilet cause a big bill?

Yes. A worn flapper can leak constantly. The food coloring test in the tank is a quick check.
