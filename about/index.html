About Findlay Water Damage Pros

A local dispatch and information service for Findlay, OH water damage, built around clear explanations and no pressure.

☎ (567) 406-9153

Findlay Water Damage Pros serves Findlay, Ohio and nearby Hancock County communities. The goal is simple: when water gets into a building, the person calling should get a clear answer fast, and should understand what happens next before anything is signed.

This site explains water damage in plain language. It covers what each type of loss involves, which factors change the work, what questions to ask a restoration company and where local public sources apply. It does not promise outcomes it cannot control.

How a call works

  1. You call the 24/7 line and describe what happened.
  2. Dispatch asks a few questions about the water, the rooms and the source.
  3. You get immediate steps to take while you wait, such as stopping the water.
  4. A crew is arranged for your address.
  5. The scope and price are explained before work starts, and changes are discussed first.

We do not quote a fixed price over the phone for damage nobody has seen, and we do not promise a specific arrival time. Both would be guesses, and water damage punishes guesses.

Where the information comes from

Local facts on this site come from public sources. The National Weather Service supplies river history and NOAA supplies climate normals. The City of Findlay supplies water and zoning information, the Ohio Department of Insurance covers coverage basics and the Ohio Revised Code covers landlord and seller duties. When a source does not answer a question, the page says to ask the relevant office.

Industry practice follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration. We refer to it where it explains why a step is done.

What dispatch asks you on the phone

Expect a short set of questions. Where is the water coming from, and has it been stopped? Which rooms are wet? Does the water look clean, gray or like sewage? Is anyone in danger, and is power involved? Those answers decide what the crew brings and what you should do in the next few minutes.

Dispatch will also tell you what not to do. Do not walk through water near live outlets. Do not run a household vacuum on sewage. Do not throw out damaged items before taking photos. Each of those rules exists because the opposite has caused harm or a disputed claim in someone's house.

How the guides on this site are written

The guides explain decisions that come after the first hour, such as insurance, estimates, permits and what to save. They are written to be useful to a homeowner, a tenant or a landlord. Where the answer depends on a policy, a lease or a local office, the guide says so and names who to ask.

We avoid pressure tactics, countdown timers and invented urgency. Water damage is urgent enough. The honest message is that water does more damage the longer it sits, and that a clear plan beats a rushed signature.

What we ask of you

Tell dispatch the truth about what happened, even if the cause was your own mistake. A washing machine hose that was overdue for replacement or a tub left running is not a reason to hesitate. The crew needs accurate information to size the drying, and your insurer will ask the same questions later.

Take photos before anything moves, keep your receipts and read what you sign. Those three habits protect you more than anything we can say on this page.

Residential and commercial work

Homes, rentals, small offices and shops all lose property to water. The steps are similar, but the paperwork differs. A homeowner deals with a homeowners policy. A landlord deals with property coverage and tenants. A business deals with downtime and records. Each service page notes where those differences matter.
