AC Leak Water Damage in Findlay, OH

An air conditioner leak is usually a clogged condensate line rather than a cooling problem. This page covers the water damage that follows and how crews dry it in Findlay homes.

☎ (567) 406-9153

AC leak water damage repair deals with what the condensate water did, not the air conditioner itself. An air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air and sends it down a drain line, and when that line clogs, water backs up and spills into the pan, the attic insulation or the ceiling below.

In Findlay, the dispatch line is open 24/7 and can arrange the dry-out while an HVAC technician fixes the equipment.

Signs an AC leak is soaking something

Look for a water stain on the ceiling below an attic air handler, dripping around a closet furnace or a damp smell when the system runs. A float switch that shut the system off is a clue that the pan filled. A slow drip from a ceiling vent can also point to condensation on duct work.

Turn the system off at the thermostat first, since a running unit will keep making water. Move belongings out from under the drip and place a bucket. Water that has reached a light fixture is an electrical hazard and needs the power turned off at the panel, if it can be reached dry.

Why condensate water spreads quietly

A condensate leak is rarely dramatic. It is a thin, steady flow that soaks attic insulation, ceiling board and framing for days or weeks. The water is usually clean at first, but standing pans and drain lines can grow slime, so the damage often smells before it shows.

Because the leak happens in the summer, closed-up houses run warm and humid, which slows drying. That is one reason a small AC leak can leave a ceiling that stays damp long after the stain stops growing.

How crews dry an AC leak

The crew traces the water with a moisture meter and thermal camera, removes wet insulation and any ceiling board that has softened and dries the framing with air movers and a dehumidifier. The HVAC contractor clears or replaces the drain line and checks the pan and float switch, since stopping the source is separate from drying.

What to do before a crew arrives

  • Turn the thermostat to off so the unit stops making more condensate.
  • Do not touch the unit or its wiring if the floor or ceiling around it is wet.
  • Set a bucket under any drip and move belongings out from under it.
  • Photograph the drain pan, the line and any stains before anything is cleaned.
  • Keep people away from a ceiling that is sagging or bulging.

Professional drying practice follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sorts water into three categories and sets drying goals by material. Ask any crew how it applies that standard to your job.

AC Leak Water Damage questions answered

Why is my air conditioner leaking water inside?

Most often the condensate drain line is clogged, or the drain pan is cracked or overflowing. An HVAC technician clears or repairs it, and restoration deals with the wet materials.

Is AC water dangerous?

Condensate is usually clean at the source, but standing water in a pan can grow slime, which causes odor. The crew checks what the water touched.

Can I keep running the system?

Turn it off until the leak is fixed, because it keeps producing water. In hot weather, ask the HVAC contractor about a temporary fix.

How do I tell if the attic insulation is wet?

Wet insulation looks flattened or darker, and the ceiling board below usually stains. A meter reading shows hidden water without tearing things out.

Does the leak need emergency service?

A sagging or dripping ceiling does. A small, dry stain can wait for a scheduled visit, though dispatch can still advise.
