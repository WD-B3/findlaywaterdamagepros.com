Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Findlay, OH

Wood floors react to water slowly and then all at once. This page covers cupping, buckling and the choice between drying and replacing hardwood and laminate in Findlay homes.

☎ (567) 406-9153

Hardwood floor water damage repair starts with a question: can the floor still be dried, or is it past that point? In Findlay, the dispatch line is open 24/7 and can arrange a moisture check. The first step is to stop the water, wipe the surface and keep the room cool and the air moving, without sanding or sealing anything yet.

Signs your wood floor has absorbed water

Cupping is when the edges of a board rise higher than the middle, and it usually appears days after a leak. Crowning is the opposite. Buckling is when boards lift off the subfloor. Dark staining, a musty smell and gaps between boards also show up. Laminate swells at the edges and the seams, and it rarely returns to its original shape.

Hardwood is slow to show damage because water enters from underneath or at the edges. A floor that looks fine on day one can start to cup on day four.

Dry it or replace it?

Timing and the amount of water decide it. Solid hardwood caught quickly can sometimes be dried with a mat system that pulls moisture through the boards, along with dehumidification, and many floors flatten again as they dry. Engineered wood is more stable but has a thin top layer. Laminate has a fiberboard core that usually swells for good once soaked.

If boards have buckled or the subfloor underneath is wet, drying in place is less likely to work. That is not a failure. It is a floor that took on more water than it could give back.

How crews dry a wood floor

The crew removes standing water, checks the boards and subfloor with a moisture meter and may use drying mats or injection panels to draw water out of the wood. Dehumidifiers pull the moisture from the room, and readings are taken each visit. Sanding or refinishing only begins after the wood reads dry, since finishing wet wood traps moisture.

What to ask before anyone sands or replaces

Wood moves slowly. A floor that cups or crowns in the days after a leak may settle as it dries, so the decision to sand, repair or replace is usually made after the moisture readings return to normal. Ask the crew to show readings from the boards and from the subfloor.

Ask also how the floor is being dried. Special drying mats and controlled airflow are used on wood so it does not dry too quickly and split. If the answer is that a fan will be pointed at it, ask for something better.

What to do before a crew arrives on a wet wood floor

  • Walk carefully, since wet wood and finish are slippery.
  • Mop up standing water and lift rugs and furniture off the floor.
  • Do not use heat guns, hair dryers or space heaters to speed drying.
  • Do not seal the floor or put plastic over it, which traps moisture.
  • Photograph cupping, staining and any gaps along the edges.

The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry's common language for water categories, loss classes and drying goals.

Hardwood Floor Water Damage questions answered

Can a cupped hardwood floor be saved?

Often, if it is dried quickly and the cupping is mild. Boards that have buckled or soaked through are less likely to recover.

How long before I can sand and refinish?

Only after the wood and subfloor read dry. The crew tells you when. Sanding wet wood can lock in problems.

What about laminate?

Laminate has a fiberboard core that swells when soaked, and it rarely recovers. Replacement is the usual outcome.

Should I take up the boards myself?

Do not, unless a crew advises it. Lifting boards at the wrong time can damage tongues and grooves.

Does the humidity in the house matter?

Yes. Drying works best in a controlled room, and the crew will set dehumidifiers and manage air flow.
