Water Damage Restoration on South Main Street, Findlay

Older homes along South Main Street often have hardwood, plaster and original basements. Here is how to handle a leak without making the damage worse.

Older homes along South Main Street

South Main Street has a number of historic houses. The Dr. Albert Linaweaver House at 1224 S. Main Street was added to the National Register of Historic Places on September 29, 1983, and the Hancock Historical Museum's 2020 historic homes tour included a Foursquare-style house at 1128 South Main.

This page is about older houses along the street in general. It does not claim that every home there is historic, and it does not describe the condition of any particular property.

Why older houses need careful drying

Homes built in the early twentieth century often have solid wood floors, plaster-and-lath or thick plaster walls, and stone or brick basements. These materials can hold moisture longer than modern drywall, and the wrong approach can lift floors or crack plaster.

Preserving original woodwork and flooring is often worth trying before replacing it, but that depends on how long the material was wet and on what the water carried. A crew can measure moisture and explain what looks salvageable.

Do this before you call

Stop the source if it is safe. Do not run power tools or operate electrical panels while standing in water. Lift small rugs and furniture off wet floors, keep pets away, and photograph the damage. If water came from a sewer line, leave it alone and call for help.

Common problems in houses like these

  • Old supply lines and valves that fail inside plaster walls.
  • Radiator, boiler and water heater leaks in basements.
  • Slow roof or flashing leaks that stain upper ceilings.
  • Damp or seeping basement walls after heavy rain.
  • Cupped or buckled hardwood after a spill or leak.

These are general patterns in older houses and are not a record of events on South Main Street. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified.

Which service fits

Wet wood floors are covered in hardwood floor water damage repair, and damaged plaster or drywall in wall and drywall water damage repair. A failed pipe is explained in burst pipe water damage.

Water that has pooled in a cellar needs basement water damage cleanup, and the moisture left behind is addressed through structural drying and dehumidification. The Findlay water damage restoration home page covers the city as a whole.

Before you call about an older home

Address

Give the street address and the rooms that are wet.

Surfaces

Say whether the floors are solid hardwood, and whether the walls are plaster or drywall.

The source

Say what you think caused it and whether it has been shut off.

Photos

Take photos before moving anything and note the date and time you found the water.

Insurance notes

Older homes may have older policies or special endorsements, and coverage depends on the policy and the cause of the loss. Nothing on this page promises payment. Contact your insurer early, and keep photos, notes and receipts together.

Mold and slow-drying materials

Plaster, wood and old insulation that stay damp can support mold, and it may hide behind walls or under floors. Fast extraction and drying lower the risk, but cannot promise that mold will not appear. Tell dispatch if you notice a musty smell.

South Main Street water damage questions

Does this page mean every South Main Street home is historic?

No. It refers to older homes along the street in general and mentions two documented examples. Whether a given house is historic is a separate question for its owner or the local preservation office.

Can hardwood floors be saved after a leak?

Sometimes. It depends on how long they stayed wet and what the water carried. A moisture check and quick drying give the best chance, but no one can promise the outcome.

Is plaster harder to dry than drywall?

Often, yes. Plaster can hold moisture longer and may need slower, careful drying. A crew can measure moisture and explain what looks workable.

What should I not do after a basement leak?

Do not walk through standing water near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, and do not operate breakers while wet. If the water is sewage, keep away and call for help.

Will insurance cover the damage?

It depends on the policy and the cause. This site cannot promise coverage, so contact your insurer directly and keep documentation.
