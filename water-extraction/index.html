Emergency Water Extraction in Findlay, OH

Pumps and extraction units pull standing water out of Findlay homes and businesses, with a dispatch line that answers day and night for the first and most time-sensitive step.

Emergency water extraction is the removal of standing water from a floor, basement or crawl space using pumps and extraction units, and in Findlay it is the first call to make when water is pooled and still spreading. The dispatch line answers 24/7. While you wait, shut off the water source, stay out of the water if any power is on, and move rugs, boxes and electronics onto dry ground.

When standing water is an emergency

Water that is moving, rising or touching electrical equipment is an emergency. So is any water that has sat for more than a few hours, because carpet, pad and drywall start absorbing it right away. A puddle you can mop in minutes is not. Two inches across a finished basement is.

Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and after-hours calls get the same priority as daytime calls. Do not wait until morning to see whether the level drops. Outside ground that stays saturated after a long rain will keep feeding a basement.

Electricity and water do not mix

If water is touching outlets, a panel or plugged-in appliances, leave the room and call the utility or 911 before you call a restoration line. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it without stepping in water.

How water is pulled out of a Findlay property

Extraction starts with the water you can see and ends with the water you cannot. Submersible pumps handle depth. Portable and truck-mounted extraction units pull water from carpet, pad and hard floors. Wet vacuums finish the corners and wall bases. Technicians then check with moisture meters, because a floor that looks dry can still be holding water underneath.

Extraction is not drying. Once the standing water is gone, the structure still holds moisture, and that is the job of air movers and dehumidifiers. The structural drying page explains the second stage, which normally starts the same day.

Where Findlay standing water comes from

Local water events arrive from plumbing, storms and the river. The National Weather Service recorded heavy rain from August 19 to 21, 2007 that took the Blanchard River near its record stage, and a house in a low block can see water arrive from the ground side long before it shows up at the curb.

A sump pump that quits during a long storm is a familiar basement trigger in any wet season.

Water that came from outside, or from a sewer line, is Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. That turns a simple pump-out into a contained cleanup, which is covered on the flood damage cleanup page and the sewage backup page.

Water Extraction questions answered

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Findlay?

Yes. The dispatch line is staffed 24/7, and after-hours calls are handled the same way as daytime calls. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and dispatch will tell you honestly what to expect when you call.

How long does extraction take?

The pump-out itself is usually measured in hours, while drying afterward is measured in days. The depth, the floor area and how many rooms are affected decide the time. A crew can give you a realistic estimate once someone has seen the water.

Can I use a shop vacuum instead?

For a small, clean spill on a hard floor, yes. A household vacuum cannot handle depth, and it does nothing for water inside carpet pad or walls. If the vacuum is electrical, never use it while standing in water.

Will carpet need to be removed after extraction?

Not always. Clean water caught quickly can sometimes be extracted from carpet and pad and the carpet dried in place. Contaminated water usually means removal. The carpet page covers how that call is made.

Is it safe to walk through standing water?

Not until you know the power situation and the water category. Water can carry contamination from drains or outside, and it can energize if it touches wiring. Stay out until the power is confirmed off or the area is cleared.
