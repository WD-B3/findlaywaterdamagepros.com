Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Findlay, OH?

Drying rarely needs a permit. Rebuilding sometimes does. The answer depends on the property type and the work, so here is who to ask.

☎ (567) 406-9153

After a water loss, most people expect a company to dry the building and then rebuild what was removed. Few expect a permit question. Yet when walls are opened, wiring touched or structure repaired, the question comes up, and a wrong guess can mean delays at sale or a problem with your insurer.

This is not legal advice and it is not a statement of every rule that applies. It is a guide to who to ask. The City of Findlay's public pages are the source for what follows, and a call to the right office is the reliable answer.

Drying and rebuilding are different stages

Extraction, drying, cleaning and temporary protection are usually mitigation work. Rebuilding, where drywall is replaced, floors are laid or cabinets are installed, is repair. Where permits come up, they tend to attach to the second stage and especially to anything involving electrical, plumbing, structure or a change of use. Ask the crew which of its work falls where, and ask the office about the repairs.

Electrical and plumbing work

If a repair includes new wiring, a replaced panel, relocated plumbing or a new water heater, ask who is allowed to do it and whether it needs a permit or inspection. Do not assume that a restoration crew holds every trade license, and do not assume that it does not. Ask for proof of what it does hold.

Why it matters later

Unpermitted work can surface at sale, in an inspection or in an insurance review. A rebuild that was documented and, where required, permitted is easier to defend. Keep a folder with the scope, the invoices, the permit numbers if any and the final photos.

A short call script

  1. State your address and whether the property is single-family, multi-family or commercial.
  2. Describe the work: drywall, flooring, electrical, plumbing, structural.
  3. Ask whether any of it needs a permit or inspection.
  4. Ask who to contact for inspections.
  5. Write down the name, date and answer.

What to ask your contractor

Ask whether the contractor expects any part of the work to need a permit, and who pulls it if so. A responsible company will tell you honestly if it does not know and will point you to the office. Be cautious of anyone who says that nothing ever needs a permit, or that you should not ask.

Ask also who is responsible if an inspector requires a change. That question is easier to settle before work starts than after drywall is closed up over wiring that someone wants to see.

If you rent or own part of a building

In a condo, townhouse or apartment, the association or the building owner may have its own rules about repairs, including who may do the work and what insurance is required. Check with the property manager before anyone cuts into a wall that could be shared.

Keep a simple permit file

If an office tells you a permit is needed, ask what it covers, what inspection comes with it and what record you will receive at the end. Keep that record with your scope and invoices.

If nothing is required, write down who told you and when. A note that says the zoning office confirmed no permit was needed on a given date is worth having if the question ever comes up again.

Permits take time, and that is one more reason to separate drying from rebuilding. Drying can start at once. The rebuild can be planned while the structure dries and the paperwork is sorted out.

What to do first while you sort out permits

Do not let a permit question delay the safety steps. Stop the water, stay out of standing water near power and photograph the damage. Drying can begin while you wait for an office to answer, and no one should cut into wiring or structure until the question is settled.

Findlay note: the offices

According to the City of Findlay's zoning page, the Zoning Office issues zoning permits, including those for change of use, new construction and floodplain management. The same page says that Wood County Building Inspection handles permitting and inspections for multi-family residential and commercial properties in Findlay and Hancock County. It does not state a rule for single-family interior repair, so do not assume either way.

The Blanchard River's floodplain matters here. The National Weather Service recorded heavy rain from August 19 to 21, 2007 that sent the river near its record stage of 18.5 feet from 1913, and properties near the river should ask the zoning office about floodplain rules before rebuilding. For rebuild questions in Van Buren, McComb or Arlington, start with the village office.
