What to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company in Findlay, OH

You often have minutes, not days, to choose. These ten questions fit on a sticky note and sort serious companies from the rest.

☎ (567) 406-9153

When water is on the floor, the first company that picks up the phone often gets the job. That is understandable, and it is also how people end up with unclear scopes and surprise invoices. Ten questions, asked in two minutes, sort most of the problems out. They also tell you how the company thinks.

None of these require technical knowledge. A good company welcomes questions because clear answers reduce disputes later. A company that gets defensive about a simple question is giving you information.

The ten questions

Ask, listen, compare

Why the answers matter

Questions 1 and 2 tell you whether the company follows recognized practice. The S500 is the published industry standard for professional water damage restoration, and asking about it is not asking for a credential. Question 3 is the most important. A written scope is the document you and your adjuster will both rely on.

Questions 4 through 6 are about transparency. A job that is documented well is easier to claim, easier to dispute and easier to finish. The structural drying page explains why daily readings matter.

Questions that protect you on price

Pricing varies with the water category, the area, the materials and the drying time. Anyone quoting a flat price for damage they have not seen is guessing. Ask what would change the number and whether you will be asked before it does.

Also ask about the order of work. Extraction and drying come first and rebuilding comes later. Signing one contract for everything on day one removes your ability to compare repair quotes. You can read how the stages break down on the water extraction page.

What a trustworthy answer to question 10 sounds like

The best companies say what they do not do. A restoration company does not usually fix the pipe, repair the roof or waterproof a foundation. It dries and rebuilds. Hearing that boundary stated plainly is reassuring because it means the company is not promising more than its trade can deliver.

How to use the list in a real call

You do not need all ten questions in a stressful moment. If the call is short, ask numbers 3, 4 and 8. A written scope, documented readings and a clear explanation of pricing tell you most of what you need. The rest can be asked when the crew arrives.

Write the answers down, with the date and the name of the person you spoke to. If you later compare two companies, notes beat memory. If your insurer asks how you chose, your notes also show you made a reasonable decision under pressure.

What a company should ask you

A good company asks questions too. It wants your address, the source, when it started, whether it is stopped, which rooms are wet and whether power is involved. Those answers tell the crew what to bring. A company that takes your number without asking any of that is not preparing for your job.

What to do first while you make calls

Before you dial, stop the water and make the space safe. Do not stand in water near outlets, and do not let a long call delay turning off a valve. Companies will wait for a minute while you do that, and the room will be much easier to deal with afterward.

Findlay note: permits and who to ask

The City of Findlay's zoning page says the City Zoning Office issues zoning permits, including for change of use, new construction and floodplain management, and that Wood County Building Inspection handles permitting and inspections for multi-family residential and commercial properties in Findlay and Hancock County. The page does not address permits for single-family interior repair, so ask the zoning office whether your rebuild needs one.

A good company will tell you honestly when a permit is likely to apply and who to ask. If you are comparing companies for a house in Arlington or McComb, ask the village or county office as well.

A local question answered

Is it rude to compare companies when water is on the floor?

No. A two-minute call to a second company is reasonable, as long as you stop the water first. A serious company will not mind.

Should I get everything in writing?

Yes. Ask for the scope and any change in price in writing, even if by email.

Who should I ask about permits in Findlay?

Start with the City of Findlay Zoning Office, and ask about building inspection for your property type. The city says Wood County Building Inspection handles multi-family and commercial permits.
