Water-Damaged Electronics: What to Unplug and What to Leave in Findlay, OH

Water and power is the one combination where waiting is risky. This guide covers what to do before you touch a single cord.

☎ (567) 406-9153

Electronics get the most attention after a water loss because they are expensive, but they are also where the danger is. Water conducts electricity, and a wet outlet, power strip or panel can injure someone long before a device is ruined. The first rule is always about people. The second rule is about devices.

This guide is general safety information, not electrical repair instruction. If there is any doubt about whether power is safe, stay out of the water and call a licensed electrician, your utility or 911.

Rule one: do not stand in water near power

If water is covering outlets, extension cords or the floor around a panel, do not walk in it. Turn off power only if the breaker panel is in a dry area and you can reach it without stepping in water.

Otherwise leave the house and call the utility or an electrician. A restoration crew will not enter standing water with live power either, and a good one will tell you so.

Rule two: do not power on a wet device

Even if a device looks dry, moisture inside circuits can cause a short when power is applied. Unplug it, remove batteries if you can do so safely, and leave it off. The urge to test whether it still works is strong, and it is the single most common way a recoverable device becomes a dead one.

Rice does not fix it

Rice is the popular home remedy for a wet phone, but it does not pull moisture from the inside of a device in any reliable way, and it can leave dust behind. Air drying in a dry room, with the device off, is better. Professional cleaning of a circuit board is the real fix, and it works best if done soon, before corrosion sets in.

Think about the building, not just the gadgets

Wet walls hide wiring. If water has run through a wall cavity, outlets and junction boxes in that wall should be inspected by an electrician before power is restored. Drying the wall with equipment is a separate job from making the wiring safe, and both must be done. The wall and drywall page explains how wet cavities are found and dried.

Document before you discard

Take photos of the model and serial label and of the damage. Your insurer may ask for a list of electronics, with their age and approximate value. Keep failed devices until your adjuster says they can go, as with other damaged contents.

After the area is safe

Once the power situation is confirmed safe, move devices out of wet rooms before the dry-out begins. Equipment on the floor, in cabinets or in low shelves is most at risk. Set it in a dry room, off the floor, with cords unplugged. Do not stack wet electronics in a closed box, because trapped moisture can finish the job that the flood started.

For anything that holds data you care about, such as a computer, a phone or a backup drive, tell a repair or data recovery professional about the water exposure before you try to power it on. Time matters because corrosion begins quickly once moisture reaches metal contacts.

Findlay note: storms and outages

The Blanchard River flooded Findlay when heavy rain fell from August 19 to 21, 2007, according to the National Weather Service, and the river approached its record stage. Floods and storms often bring both outages and surges. Power returning after an outage is a risky moment for wet equipment, so keep devices unplugged until the area has been checked.

The 811 line, run in Ohio by the Ohio Utilities Protection Service at 1-800-362-2764, is for marking buried lines before digging, not for emergencies in the house. For a power emergency, call 911 or your electric utility. Homeowners in McComb and Van Buren should follow the same rule.

A local question answered

Should I turn on a wet TV to see if it works?

No. Unplug it, keep it dry and have it inspected. Powering on a wet device can cause a short.

Is it safe to flip the breaker in a wet basement?

Only if the panel is in a dry area and you do not have to step in water. If not, leave and call the utility or an electrician.

Does the 811 number help with an electrical emergency?

No. 811 is for marking buried utilities before digging. Call 911 or your electric utility for emergencies.
