Downtown Findlay Water Damage Restoration

Older storefronts, upstairs apartments and basements around Courthouse Square dry out differently from newer buildings. Here is what to do first, and who to call.

☎ (567) 406-9153

What makes downtown buildings different

Downtown Findlay is the older commercial core of the city. The Findlay Downtown Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on February 28, 1985, and the listing records 263 contributing buildings, roughly along Main Street, West Sandusky Street and West Main Cross Street.

Buildings like these often have storefronts at street level, offices or apartments above, and a basement below. Water usually travels through shared walls and ceilings into the next level, so a small leak can involve several tenants.

Common water problems in older mixed-use buildings

  • Roof drains and flat-roof ponding that leak into upper ceilings.
  • Aging supply and drain lines inside shared walls.
  • Basement seepage and floor-drain backups after sustained rain.
  • Sprinkler or heating piping that releases water across several rooms.

These are general patterns, not a record of any specific downtown event.

The Blanchard River and downtown flooding

Findlay sits on the Blanchard River, and the National Weather Service reports major flooding in the city on August 19 to 21, 2007, with the river near its record stage. The same office lists the record flood as 18.5 feet, in 1913.

That history is about the city as a whole. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. For any specific downtown property, check the current flood maps for that exact address.

If water is spreading right now

Stop the source if it is safe. Do not touch electrical panels, outlets or switches while standing in water, and leave the building if water is near live equipment. Sewage or river water is not a do-it-yourself cleanup, so keep people and pets away and call for help.

Which service fits

A tenant or owner with a wet storefront or office usually needs commercial water damage restoration. A flooded cellar starts with water extraction, and the wet materials then need structural drying so moisture does not stay inside walls and floors.

If a supply line failed inside a wall, read about burst pipe water damage for the first steps. The wider picture for the city is on the Findlay water damage restoration home page.

Before you call about a downtown building

Street address and floor

Give the address and say which levels are wet, including any basement.

Who owns or manages it

Say whether you are the owner, a tenant or a property manager, since access and approvals differ.

The source

Say what you think caused it, and whether the water has been shut off.

Photos and notes

Take photos of wet areas, damaged stock and equipment, and note the time you found it.

Insurance and documentation

Commercial and residential policies differ, and what is covered depends on the policy and the cause of the loss. Nothing on this page promises coverage. Tell your insurer or broker early and keep photos and receipts together.

If repairs need a permit, note that the City of Findlay's zoning page says Wood County Building Inspection handles permitting and inspection for multi-family residential and commercial properties in Findlay and Hancock County.

Mold and drying time

Wet drywall, insulation and wood can grow mold if they stay damp, and older buildings can hide moisture in wall cavities. Quick drying lowers the risk but cannot promise that mold will not appear. Mention any mold smell when you call.

Sources: National Park Service, National Register Information System, record 85000402; National Weather Service Cleveland, August 2007 flood summary; City of Findlay zoning page.

Downtown Findlay water damage questions

Is Downtown Findlay in the same flood zone as the rest of the city?

That cannot be answered for a whole district. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the current FEMA flood map for the exact address, and ask your insurer what applies to the property.

Who do I call for a downtown storefront or upstairs apartment leak?

Call the number on this page, say which floors are wet and whether you own or rent. Dispatch will explain the likely service and what to expect. The building owner or manager may also need to be told.

Can water from one floor damage the units below?

Yes. In multi-level buildings water often follows pipes, walls and ceilings into other units. Photograph every affected area and tell the owner and your insurer, since more than one party may be involved.

Should I clean up sewage or river water myself?

No. Sewage backup and floodwater can carry bacteria and other contaminants. Keep people away, avoid contact, and call for professional help.

Will my insurance pay for the cleanup?

It depends on the policy and the cause. Sudden pipe failures are often treated differently from flood or groundwater damage. This site cannot promise coverage, so ask your insurer directly.
